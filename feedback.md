# Review Notes: CSF Letter to State Street Properties

## Overall impression

The letter is credible, respectful, and well-intentioned. It clearly reflects CSF’s mission around creative placemaking and the need for affordable artist space in Fredericton.

The main issue is not basic legibility, but **strategic readability**. The letter takes a long time to reach the main request, and some sentences are dense enough that a busy property owner may skim past the strongest points.

## Main recommendations

### 1. Put the request near the beginning

The ask should be clear in the first paragraph:

> CSF is asking State Street Properties to consider a temporary hold on leasing the Queen Street Post “pit space” while CSF applies for funding to complete a feasibility study for its possible conversion into a downtown artist hub.

This lets the reader immediately understand what decision is being requested.

### 2. Make the request time-limited

The current request could feel open-ended from a business perspective. A defined time period would likely be easier to accept.

Suggested wording:

> Would State Street Properties consider not actively leasing the “pit space” to another party for a defined period, such as 90–120 days, while CSF pursues feasibility-study funding and continues discussions with City staff?

This makes the request feel reasonable and lower-risk.

### 3. Shorten the background

The history is useful, but there is too much of it before the main request. Consider reducing the background to only the points needed to support the ask:

* Fredericton artists need affordable studio and collaborative space.
* QSP was identified as a strong candidate.
* Connexion ARC expressed interest in the space.
* The City has shown interest in creative placemaking and may be a potential partner.
* A feasibility study is the next practical step.

### 4. Emphasize the benefit to State Street

The letter should more clearly explain why State Street might want to support this:

* Potential municipal lease pathway.
* Positive civic leadership.
* Downtown activation.
* Alignment with the City’s Creative City Strategy.
* Productive use of a difficult or unusual space.
* Goodwill with the arts, cultural, and downtown communities.

### 5. Reduce sentence length

Several sentences are long and contain multiple ideas. Shorter paragraphs would make the letter easier to read and more persuasive.

Aim for paragraphs of 3–5 sentences, with one main point per paragraph.

## Layout and presentation notes

* Remove “SECOND DRAFT” before sending.
* Remove the extra blank page, if present.
* Consider reducing the letter to two pages.
* Add page numbers or a subtle footer on pages 2–3.
* Darken the grey contact information slightly for better print readability.
* Keep “creative placemaking” consistent throughout; avoid switching between “creative place-making” and “creative placemaking.”

## Suggested stronger closing ask

> We recognize that this request has business implications, and we would welcome the chance to discuss a reasonable time frame, such as 90–120 days, or another arrangement that would protect State Street’s interests while allowing this civic opportunity to be properly assessed.
>
> In our view, there is no stronger downtown candidate space for this purpose. Its location between Officer’s Square, the Fredericton Night Market, and NBCCD gives it unusual potential to support artists, activate downtown, and align with the City’s emerging Creative City Strategy.
>
> Thank you for considering this request. We would be pleased to meet at your convenience to discuss the proposal and timeline in more detail.

## Bottom line

The letter has the right substance. It would be stronger if it were **shorter, more direct, time-bounded, and clearer about the benefit to State Street Properties**.
