# Spy waveform presentation

Date: 2026-10-27

## Context

Wave-reference assets already show per-pixel waveform crossing density. Spies
previously offered only cyclograms and spectrograms.

## Decisions

- Offer a third, time-domain Waveform presentation for Spies, with crossing
  counts mapped to an existing heat gradient such as Burnt Alum.
- Include zooming and scrolling. Reuse reference-waveform logic to switch to a
  sample path when visible zero crossings are sparse relative to pixel width.
- Preserve the rich overlap colours of the compact Spy when expanding: use
  coarse cells for heat statistics and fine cells for waveform coverage. Dark
  Burnt Alum blue against the grey canvas needs better visual contrast.
- Use somewhat finer heat buckets with smooth colour transitions. Anchor heat
  statistics and their normalization to the full signal so zooming retains the
  colour of the original region, including after switching to a sample path.
- After visual review, double the heat grid to 768 by 384 and use a different,
  brighter gradient. Sparse traces need clear contrast against the grey plot;
  smoothing or stable colouring alone does not solve low luminance contrast.
- A uniformly bright ramp is insufficient: density bands need visibly distinct
  colours. Offer gradient choices instead of truncating a palette to raise its
  brightness.
- Consolidate time surface, bipolar spectral surface and waveform density
  choices in one modal configurator, with real gradient strips in the selectors
  and their menus. Save choices with the preset and preview them immediately.
- Keep appearance menus focused: Time has Blues, Icy Hot (Raw), Greyscale;
  bipolar magnitude has Icy Hot (Raw), Blue / Amber; waveform density has Burnt
  Alum, Blues, Greyscale. Unipolar magnitude stays Burnt Alum; bipolar phase
  retains its current palette.
- Use black backgrounds and matching major/minor grids for expanded waveform
  and time-domain 2D plots. Keep live zoom/scroll responsive; distinguish native
  GPU drawing/composition from CPU crossing analysis and coverage generation.
- At sufficiently resolved waveform zoom, add a translucent bipolar fill to
  the zero line and a slightly thicker edge stroke, like the Trimesh waveform
  view. Preserve selected heat colours in both fill and stroke.
- Reduce phase-dependent bucket striping by filtering counts before palette
  lookup and considering fractional trace coverage, preserving source anchoring.
- Optimize crossing counts and cache the source/index; zoom and repaint must
  not recapture audio or rebuild unrelated state.

## Generalized Rules

Reuse the reference waveform presentation for equivalent time-signal views,
including its zoom-dependent rendering, rather than duplicating the algorithm.
